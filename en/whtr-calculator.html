What is waist-to-height ratio?

Waist-to-height ratio (WHtR) is your waist circumference divided by your height, in the same unit. Its strength is that it measures where fat is stored, not just total weight. Visceral fat inside the abdomen, around the liver and intestines, is metabolically active, releases inflammatory substances and is strongly linked to insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, fatty liver, high blood pressure and heart disease.

Two people with the same BMI can therefore carry very different risks: one stores fat on the hips, the other around the belly. A meta-analysis in Obesity Reviews concluded that waist-to-height ratio is a better screening tool than waist circumference or BMI for adult cardiometabolic risk factors.

How is the ratio calculated?

Waist-to-height ratio = waist circumference (cm) ÷ height (cm)

Worked example: someone who is 175 cm tall with a 94 cm waist: 94 ÷ 175 = 0.54, in the increased-risk range. Their healthy waist limit is half their height, 175 ÷ 2 = 87.5 cm, so they would need to lose about 6.5 cm from their waist.

A big advantage is that the healthy boundary is roughly the same for men and women, different ethnic groups and different heights, as a systematic review in Nutrition Research Reviews showed when it proposed 0.5 as a global boundary value.

How to measure your waist accurately

  1. Stand up straight with your feet close together and lift your clothing so the tape sits directly on your skin.
  2. Find the bottom of your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone on your side, and place the tape halfway between them, as WHO and NICE recommend.
  3. Keep the tape horizontal around your body, touching the skin without compressing it.
  4. Breathe in normally, breathe out, and read the measurement at the end of the out-breath without pulling your stomach in.
  5. Measure twice and take the average. To track change, measure at the same time of day (in the morning before eating).

If you cannot find the bones, measuring at navel level is a common practical alternative; just use the same method every time.

For comparison, WHO uses waist circumference alone: increased risk above 94 cm for men and 80 cm for women, and substantially increased risk above 102 cm for men and 88 cm for women. Those cut-offs ignore height, so a tall and a short man are judged by the same number, which is where waist-to-height ratio does better.

WHtR, WHR or BMI?

  • Body mass index (BMI): reflects overall body size but not where fat sits. Calculate it with the BMI calculator.
  • Waist-to-hip ratio (WHR): a different measure that divides the waist by the hip circumference. It needs two measurements and has different cut-offs for men and women.
  • Waist-to-height ratio (WHtR): one waist measurement and roughly one boundary for everyone (0.5). That is why NICE recommended it in 2022 alongside BMI for adults with a BMI below 35.

An international consensus statement in Nature Reviews Endocrinology (2020) called for waist circumference to be treated as a “vital sign”, recorded at every clinical visit like blood pressure.

When is the ratio less reliable?

  • Pregnancy: do not use it during pregnancy.
  • Children and teenagers: the NICE boundaries apply to adults; children are assessed with their doctor.
  • Abdominal swelling for medical reasons: such as ascites, a hernia or severe bloating.
  • BMI of 35 or more: the risk is already high, and the extra measurement changes the classification little.

How to reduce visceral belly fat: evidence-based tips

The good news is that visceral fat often responds faster than fat under the skin. Sit-ups alone strengthen the muscles but do not burn fat from one specific spot; the answer is whole-body change:

  1. A moderate calorie deficit: work it out with the TDEE calculator. Losing 5–10% of body weight clearly reduces belly fat.
  2. Regular aerobic activity: 150 to 300 minutes a week of brisk walking, swimming or cycling, plus resistance training two days a week.
  3. Cut added sugar, sugary drinks and refined starches: choose low-GI, high-fibre foods instead.
  4. Protein and fibre at every meal: they keep you full and protect muscle during weight loss.
  5. Sleep and stress: short sleep and chronically high cortisol are linked to belly-fat build-up; aim for at least 7 hours of sleep.
  6. Check your blood sugar: if your ratio is 0.5 or more, ask your doctor for HbA1c, lipids and liver enzymes.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy waist-to-height ratio?

0.4 to 0.49 for adults, meaning your waist is less than half your height. 0.5 to 0.59 means increased health risk, and 0.6 or more means further increased risk according to NICE 2022.

Where exactly should I measure my waist?

Halfway between the bottom of your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone, with the tape horizontal on bare skin, at the end of a normal out-breath. Measuring at the navel is a practical alternative if you use it consistently.

Is waist-to-height ratio better than BMI?

For flagging diabetes and heart-disease risk, yes in many studies, because it captures belly fat. Using both together, as NICE recommends, is best.

What is the difference between WHtR and WHR?

WHtR divides your waist by your height and has a healthy boundary of about 0.5 for everyone. WHR divides your waist by your hip circumference, needs two measurements and has different cut-offs for men and women.

Do sit-ups reduce belly fat?

They strengthen your abdominal muscles but do not burn fat specifically from your belly. Belly fat goes down with a calorie deficit, aerobic activity, resistance training, better sleep and a better diet.

Can children use this calculator?

The boundaries shown here are for adults under NICE guidance. Children’s weight and waist are assessed with their doctor using age-appropriate standards.
