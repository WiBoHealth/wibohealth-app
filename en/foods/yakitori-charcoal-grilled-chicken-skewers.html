Yakitori is the Japanese art of grilling chicken over binchotan charcoal. Pieces of chicken breast or thigh are threaded onto bamboo skewers with pieces of spring onion (negi) and grilled over fast, dry heat. This lets some of the fat drip away, leaving tender chicken with 24g of protein per 100g, moderate calories and controlled fat (8g). It is an excellent choice if you want filling protein and support for muscle mass without fried food.

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

Two yakitori skewers (about 120g) make an excellent protein meal for lunch or dinner with a green salad dressed in olive oil.

Best preparation methods

In Japanese restaurants, order yakitori with salt (shio) instead of tare sauce: salt seasoning adds almost no sugar, while the sauce adds refined sugar.

Important Nutritional Note

Tare sauce is traditionally made from soy sauce with mirin or sake (rice wine), so if you eat halal, order salt-seasoned skewers only (yakitori shio) and check that the chicken itself is halal.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Yakitori Chicken Skewers good for weight loss?

    It can be a useful part of a weight-loss plan because it provides 13 g of protein per 100 kcal, and protein supports fullness. A 120 g portion gives 222 kcal.

    How does Yakitori Chicken Skewers affect blood sugar?

    Yakitori Chicken Skewers contains only 4.3 g of available carbohydrate per 100 g, so the glycemic index does not apply and its direct effect on blood sugar is minimal. The meal’s effect depends on what you eat with it, such as bread, rice or sweetened sauces.

    What is the nutritional value of a typical portion of Yakitori Chicken Skewers?

    A typical 120 g portion of Yakitori Chicken Skewers provides about 222 kcal, 28.8 g protein, 5.4 g carbohydrate, 9.6 g fat and 0.2 g fibre.

    Do yakitori skewers raise blood sugar?

    Salt-grilled skewers (shio) have a very small effect on blood sugar (the glycemic index does not apply) and suit keto. Skewers brushed with sweet tare sauce add about 2 to 3g of carbohydrate each.

    Is yakitori good for athletes and cutting diets?

    Yes, it is an excellent option: high in easily digested protein, and chicken is a source of vitamin B6 and niacin, which contribute to normal energy-yielding metabolism.

    Does charcoal grilling carry health risks?

    Grilling directly over flames can form compounds such as heterocyclic amines and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, so trim off any blackened edges and eat the meal with leafy vegetables.
