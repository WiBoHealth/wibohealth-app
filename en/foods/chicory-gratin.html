Belgian Chicory Gratin with Turkey Ham

Witloof in de oven met kalkoenhamSoups & home-cooked dishes

Chicory, or Belgian endive (witloof), is the pride of the farms of Belgium and northern France, and its pleasant mild bitterness comes from natural plant compounds. In this dish the chicory heads are steamed, wrapped in slices of turkey ham, covered with a light Gouda cheese sauce and baked until golden. The result is a winter meal with good protein (about 11.5 g per 100 g) and very little available carbohydrate (about 3.2 g per 100 g), so the glycemic index does not apply and it barely raises blood sugar. Chicory itself is low in calories and provides fibre that benefits gut bacteria.

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

Two wrapped chicory heads (220 g, about 286 kcal).

Best preparation methods

Steam the chicory well and press it gently before wrapping so the dish does not turn watery, make a light cheese sauce with semi-skimmed milk, and serve with a green salad.

Important Nutritional Note

Processed turkey ham and cheese add a noticeable amount of salt, so people with high blood pressure should eat it in moderation and add no salt to the sauce. Ingredient note: the traditional Belgian and French recipe uses cooked pork ham (hesp); our version uses turkey ham.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Belgian Chicory Gratin with Turkey Ham good for weight loss?

    It can be a useful part of a weight-loss plan because it provides 8.8 g of protein per 100 kcal, and protein supports fullness. A 220 g portion gives 286 kcal.

    How does Belgian Chicory Gratin with Turkey Ham affect blood sugar?

    Belgian Chicory Gratin with Turkey Ham contains only 3.2 g of available carbohydrate per 100 g, so the glycemic index does not apply and its direct effect on blood sugar is minimal. The meal’s effect depends on what you eat with it, such as bread, rice or sweetened sauces.

    What is the nutritional value of a typical portion of Belgian Chicory Gratin with Turkey Ham?

    A typical 220 g portion of Belgian Chicory Gratin with Turkey Ham provides about 286 kcal, 25.3 g protein, 13.2 g carbohydrate, 16.5 g fat and 6.2 g fibre.

    Why is Belgian chicory a good choice for people with diabetes?

    Because it is almost sugar-free and very low in calories, and its fibre adds bulk to the meal and helps you feel full. In this dish the turkey ham and cheese add protein, giving you a filling meal that hardly raises blood sugar.

    How do you get rid of excess bitterness in chicory?

    Cut out the small cone at the base of the head with a knife before cooking, as it holds most of the bitterness, and steam it with a squeeze of lemon and some nutmeg.

    Does it suit older people and the DASH diet for blood pressure?

    Chicory itself is excellent for DASH, but processed turkey ham and the cheese sauce raise the salt. Choose low-salt turkey ham, use a moderate amount of cheese and add no salt to make the dish a good fit.
