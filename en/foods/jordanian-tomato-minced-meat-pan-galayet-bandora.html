Jordanian Tomato & Minced Meat Pan (Galayet Bandora)

Quick answer

A medium portion of Jordanian Tomato & Minced Meat Pan (Galayet Bandora) (200 g) contains 370 kcal, with 10.8 g of net carbohydrates and 28 g of protein. Its glycemic index is 25 (Low) for how fast it raises blood sugar, while its glycemic load per portion is 2.7 (Low) for its actual effect.

The best-loved skillet dish of the Jordanian countryside: local tomatoes simmered over the fire with minced meat, virgin olive oil, hot pepper and garlic; a meal rich in the antioxidant lycopene with a minimal glycemic load (2.7).

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

Cooking tomatoes with olive oil clearly increases the absorption of the antioxidant lycopene.

Best preparation methods

Eat it with a spoon or with wholegrain bread instead of white bread to avoid raising the glycemic load of the meal.

Important Nutritional Note

For people with diabetes or insulin resistance, it is always recommended to consider the total glycemic load (GL) of the meal rather than relying on the glycemic index alone.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Jordanian Tomato & Minced Meat Pan (Galayet Bandora) good for weight loss?

    It can fit a balanced plan with portion control: a 200 g portion gives 370 kcal, and what matters most is your total daily intake and the rest of the meal.

    How does Jordanian Tomato & Minced Meat Pan (Galayet Bandora) affect blood sugar?

    Its glycemic index is 25 (Low), a clinically reviewed estimate rather than a laboratory measurement. A typical 200 g portion has a glycemic load of 2.7 (Low), so the effect of this portion on blood sugar is limited.

    What is the nutritional value of a typical portion of Jordanian Tomato & Minced Meat Pan (Galayet Bandora)?

    A typical 200 g portion of Jordanian Tomato & Minced Meat Pan (Galayet Bandora) provides about 370 kcal, 28 g protein, 15 g carbohydrate, 22 g fat and 4.2 g fibre.
