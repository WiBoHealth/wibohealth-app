Pecorino Romano

Pecorino Romano schapenkaasDairy & cheese

Quick answer

A medium portion of Pecorino Romano (10 g) contains 39 kcal, with 0.2 g of net carbohydrates and 3.2 g of protein.

The ancient, historic cheese of Rome: made only from whole sheep's milk, salted and aged until it is hard, with a sharp, peppery, salty flavour. It contains almost no carbohydrate, so a glycemic index does not apply, and it is the cornerstone of classic Roman pasta dishes such as carbonara and cacio e pepe.

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

A small sprinkle of grated pecorino is enough to give a dish a sharp flavour; use it as a smart way to need less ordinary salt while adding healthy fats and natural calcium.

Best preparation methods

Mix it grated with freshly ground black pepper and sprinkle it over roasted vegetables or egg dishes to lift both the flavour and the fullness.

Important Nutritional Note

Sheep's milk is rich in conjugated linoleic acid (CLA), which benefits the blood vessels; because it is naturally very salty, people with high blood pressure should keep the amount moderate.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Pecorino Romano good for weight loss?

    It can be a useful part of a weight-loss plan because it provides 8.3 g of protein per 100 kcal, and protein supports fullness. A 10 g portion gives 39 kcal.

    How does Pecorino Romano affect blood sugar?

    Pecorino Romano contains only 2 g of available carbohydrate per 100 g, so the glycemic index does not apply and its direct effect on blood sugar is minimal. The meal’s effect depends on what you eat with it, such as bread, rice or sweetened sauces.

    What is the nutritional value of a typical portion of Pecorino Romano?

    A typical 10 g portion of Pecorino Romano provides about 39 kcal, 3.2 g protein, 0.2 g carbohydrate, 2.8 g fat and 0 g fibre.
