What is monk fruit?

A small, round green fruit from southern China called Luo Han Guo. Buddhist monks grew it for hundreds of years, and that is where its name comes from 😄 Its sweetness comes from substances called mogrosides, which are about 150 to 250 times sweeter than sugar.

How many calories does it have, and does it raise blood sugar?

The pure extract has no calories and does not raise blood sugar, because the amount you use is very small and mogrosides are not turned into sugar in the blood. So the glycemic index does not apply.

Why is it hard to find in Europe?

Here is the story: in the US it has been allowed for years as a sweetener, and you see it in every video. But in the European Union, its legal status is limited and complicated, and the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) has asked for additional safety studies. That is why you won't find it as easily in supermarkets here as stevia.

Watch out for blends

Many "monk fruit" products in the US are mostly erythritol with a little monk fruit extract. So read the ingredients: if the first ingredient is erythritol, you are actually using erythritol. Read also: Erythritol (Sweetener)

If you live in Europe, what is the alternative?

  • Stevia: from a plant, no calories, and permitted (E960). Read also: Liquid Stevia Drops
  • Erythritol: its sweetness is close to sugar, and it is permitted (E968).
  • Sucralose: no calories, and permitted (E955).

Frequently asked questions

Does monk fruit raise blood sugar?

No, the pure extract does not raise blood sugar and has no calories.

Is monk fruit allowed in Europe?

Its status in the European Union is limited and complicated, so you won't find it as easily as stevia.

What is the difference between monk fruit and stevia?

Both are plant-based sweeteners with no calories. Stevia comes from the leaves of a plant and is permitted in Europe, while monk fruit comes from a fruit and its status here is limited.

Why do monk fruit products contain erythritol?

Because the extract is very sweet, so it is mixed with erythritol to give it the same volume and sweetness as sugar.
