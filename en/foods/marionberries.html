Marionberries

MarionbessenFruit

Quick answer

A medium portion of Marionberries (125 g) contains 60 kcal, with 8.4 g of net carbohydrates and 1.5 g of protein. Its glycemic index is 25 (Low) for how fast it raises blood sugar, while its glycemic load per portion is 2.1 (Low) for its actual effect.

Developed in Oregon, USA, to become an icon of quality, chefs even call it the "Cabernet of blackberries", comparing it with the finest grapes, because of its complex flavour that blends the sweetness of wild blackberry with light earthy notes and well-balanced acidity. The berries are long and glossy and the flesh melts smoothly. One cup (125 g) gives you about 6.4 g of fibre and only 60 kcal, with a low estimated glycemic index (25) and a glycemic load of 2.1, so your energy stays steady for hours.

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

A full cup (125 g). A great mid-day snack for a natural lift and focus.

Best preparation methods

Mix them with a small handful of raw walnuts or a sprinkle of hemp seeds: the healthy fat and protein make the snack more filling and calm the absorption of sugar.

Important Nutritional Note

The seeds are very small and do not bother your teeth, and together with the skin they contain antioxidant compounds such as ellagic acid. Chew the berries well to benefit, and wash them only just before eating.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Marionberries good for weight loss?

    Yes. Marionberries is very low in calories (48 kcal per 100 g), so you can eat a generous amount to add volume and fullness to a meal without many calories.

    How does Marionberries affect blood sugar?

    Its glycemic index is 25 (Low), a clinically reviewed estimate rather than a laboratory measurement. A typical 125 g portion has a glycemic load of 2.1 (Low), so the effect of this portion on blood sugar is limited.

    What is the nutritional value of a typical portion of Marionberries?

    A typical 125 g portion of Marionberries provides about 60 kcal, 1.5 g protein, 14.8 g carbohydrate, 0.5 g fat and 6.4 g fibre.

    What makes marionberries taste different from ordinary blackberries?

    Ordinary blackberries can taste watery or harshly sour if not fully ripe, while marionberries have a honeyed, balanced sweetness and a thin skin that melts in the mouth.

    What does the ellagic acid in marionberries do?

    Gut bacteria turn it into compounds called urolithins, which researchers are studying for their possible role in cell health and inflammation. Early results are encouraging, but their effect on insulin resistance in people is not settled yet.

    How do I keep marionberries fresh for as long as possible?

    Put a dry paper towel in the bottom of a container, lay the berries in a single layer without piling them up, keep them dry on the coldest shelf of the fridge, and wash them only just before eating.
