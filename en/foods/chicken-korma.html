Chicken korma is the aristocrat of Mughal cooking in India: tender chicken in a velvety ivory sauce rich in yoghurt, almond and cashew paste and gentle spices such as cardamom and nutmeg. Its glycemic index does not apply because it contains almost no net starch (about 2.5 g per 100 g), and it is a refined dish that is gentle on the stomach.

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

Eat the chicken with its nut sauce with a spoon; the healthy fats and protein keep you full for a long time without white naan.

Best preparation methods

Korma gets its texture from cashew and almond paste and yoghurt, with no flour or starch, which makes it a healthy dish rich in monounsaturated fats.

Important Nutritional Note

Cashews and almonds provide magnesium and heart-protective fats, but the dish is calorie-rich and the portion needs watching in weight-loss plans.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is chicken korma as hot as other Indian curries?

    Not at all: korma is a very mild, creamy dish without chilli heat, loved by children and by people sensitive to spicy food.

    What is the nutritional value of a 250 g portion of chicken korma?

    The portion contains about 360 kcal, 32 g of protein, 24 g of beneficial fat from the nuts and only about 6 g of carbohydrate.

    Is chicken korma suitable for people with diabetes?

    Absolutely: it does not raise blood sugar, and the nut fats and protein keep insulin completely steady.
