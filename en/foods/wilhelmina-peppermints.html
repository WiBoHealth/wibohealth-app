Wilhelmina Peppermint Lozenges

Sweets & desserts

Quick answer

A medium portion of Wilhelmina Peppermint Lozenges (6 g) contains 24 kcal, with 5.9 g of net carbohydrates and 0 g of protein. Its glycemic index is 70 (High) for how fast it raises blood sugar, while its glycemic load per portion is 4.1 (Low) for its actual effect.

Serving advice

Don't have more than one mint, and only when you really need fresh breath. Don't make it a habit throughout the working day, because constantly sucking on sugar keeps insulin raised.

Best ways to prepare it

Swap them for a cup of fresh mint tea, brewed without sugar, or chew a few green mint leaves for the aromatic oils and antioxidants without the sugar.

Important nutrition note ⚠️

They break an intermittent fast straight away because they trigger insulin release. Sucking them often is also a real risk to tooth enamel and feeds the mouth bacteria that cause cavities.

Frequently asked questions

Does a Wilhelmina mint raise blood sugar in people with diabetes?

Yes. Each mint is 3 grams of pure sucrose with a high glycemic index (70). One mint won't cause a big shock, but 3 or 4 in a row will push your blood sugar reading up quickly.

Do peppermints fit keto or intermittent fasting?

They suit neither keto nor the fasting window: they are pure sugar and can take the body out of ketosis.

What sugar-free alternative is available in Dutch and European shops?

Look for sugar-free versions sweetened with xylitol or stevia, and read the ingredients to make sure they contain no glucose syrup. Try the Boodi app 💚 to check a product's ingredients and find lower-sugar alternatives.
