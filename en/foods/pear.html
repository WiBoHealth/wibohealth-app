Pear

PeerFruit

Quick answer

A medium portion of Pear (170 g) contains 97 kcal, with 20.2 g of net carbohydrates and 0.7 g of protein. Its glycemic index is 36 (Low) for how fast it raises blood sugar, while its glycemic load per portion is 7.3 (Low) for its actual effect.

The pear is a loyal friend for people with diabetes and insulin resistance: its glycemic index is very low (36), and its sweet taste comes with plenty of water and soluble fibre that give long-lasting fullness and steady blood sugar.

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

An excellent everyday fruit between meals that cuts off sudden hunger pangs, and its pectin fibre supports good digestion and gut health.

Best preparation methods

Eat the whole pear with its antioxidant-rich skin after washing it well, and avoid canned pears in sugary syrup.

Important Nutritional Note

For people with diabetes or insulin resistance, it is always recommended to consider the total glycemic load (GL) of the meal rather than relying on the glycemic index alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is Pear good for weight loss?

Yes. Pear is very low in calories (57 kcal per 100 g), so you can eat a generous amount to add volume and fullness to a meal without many calories.

How does Pear affect blood sugar?

Its glycemic index is 36 (Low) according to Atkinson 2021. A typical 170 g portion has a glycemic load of 7.3 (Low), so the effect of this portion on blood sugar is limited.

What is the nutritional value of a typical portion of Pear?

A typical 170 g portion of Pear provides about 97 kcal, 0.7 g protein, 25.5 g carbohydrate, 0.2 g fat and 5.3 g fibre.
