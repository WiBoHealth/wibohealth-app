What is allulose?

Allulose, or "the rare sugar", is a kind of sugar found naturally in very small amounts in figs, raisins and maple. Its taste and texture are very close to ordinary sugar, and it is about 70% as sweet as sugar. That is why it became the star of American and keto videos.

How many calories does it have, and does it raise blood sugar?

The body absorbs it but hardly burns it, and most of it leaves in the urine. So its calories are very low: according to the page's data, 20 calories per 100 g, so a teaspoon has about one calorie.

Studies say that it hardly raises blood sugar. But there is no official measurement of its glycemic index using the approved international method, so we will not give you a number.

Why is it still not allowed in Europe?

This is the most important thing to know if you live in Europe: allulose is not yet allowed as a food in the European Union. The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) reviewed the file and said in 2025 that the safety studies submitted were not sufficient for approval. So it is allowed in the US, but not here. If you see it online, be aware that it is not approved here.

What is the alternative in Europe?

  • Erythritol: the closest thing to allulose in taste and texture, and permitted. Read also: Erythritol (Sweetener)
  • Stevia: no calories, and permitted.
  • Sucralose: no calories, and it tolerates heat.
