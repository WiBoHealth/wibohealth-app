The shining pearl of Greece, with a deep purple colour, almond shape and rich, fruity flavour, full of oleic acid and antioxidants such as hydroxytyrosol. Almost free of carbohydrate, the glycemic index does not apply, and they are a cornerstone of the Mediterranean diet and heart health.

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

Add 4 or 5 olives to your daily green salad or breakfast plate; their monounsaturated fats help slow carbohydrate digestion and support lasting fullness.

Best preparation methods

Choose olives preserved in extra virgin olive oil and vinegar rather than ones treated with caustic soda, and soak them in lukewarm water before serving to reduce the excess sodium.

Important Nutritional Note

Kalamata olives are a nutritional treasure that supports flexible blood vessels and healthy HDL cholesterol; a moderate daily amount gives energy and fullness with no burden on blood sugar.

Frequently asked questions

Are Kalamata Olives good for weight loss?

It can be included in moderation: it contains 3.2 g of fibre per 100 g, which supports fullness, and a 20 g portion gives 23 kcal.

How do Kalamata Olives affect blood sugar?

Kalamata Olives contain only 3.1 g of available carbohydrate per 100 g, so the glycemic index does not apply and its direct effect on blood sugar is minimal. The meal’s effect depends on what you eat with it, such as bread, rice or sweetened sauces.

What is the nutritional value of a typical portion of Kalamata Olives?

A typical 20 g portion of Kalamata Olives provides about 23 kcal, 0.2 g protein, 1.3 g carbohydrate, 2.1 g fat and 0.6 g fibre.
