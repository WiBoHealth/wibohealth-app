Kung Pao Chicken with Peanuts & Chilies

Kung Pao kip met pinda's en Sichuan pepersAsian dishes

Quick answer

A medium portion of Kung Pao Chicken with Peanuts & Chilies (200 g) contains 480 kcal, with 17.8 g of net carbohydrates and 44 g of protein. Its glycemic index is 45 (Low) for how fast it raises blood sugar, while its glycemic load per portion is 8 (Low) for its actual effect.

The king of spicy Sichuan cooking: a lively classic that tosses tender chicken pieces with roasted peanuts and the tingle of Sichuan pepper, which leaves the mouth warm and refreshed. A meal that brings energy and plenty of protein without piling on starch.

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

A complete meal on its own; serve it with a crisp green salad or a fist-sized portion of boiled basmati rice instead of oily fried rice.

Best preparation methods

Cook it in a wok using the quick stir-fry method with just one spoon of sesame oil, and avoid too much cornstarch in the marinade or any refined sugar.

Important Nutritional Note

Its glycemic index is low (45) and its glycemic load is low (8 per 200 g portion), making it excellent for building muscle and keeping blood sugar steady; people with high blood pressure should watch the sodium from the soy sauce.

    Frequently asked questions

    How many calories are in a plate of Kung Pao chicken?

    A 200 g home-made portion cooked with little oil has about 480 kcal and is very rich in lean protein (44 g).

    Is it suitable for keto and insulin resistance?

    Yes, very much so, as long as it is made without added sugar or honey in the sauce, because it relies on the healthy fat of peanuts and the protein of chicken.

    How do the peanuts in the dish affect blood sugar?

    The healthy fat and fibre in peanuts slow down the digestion of the meal considerably, protecting against sudden sugar dips and keeping you full for hours.
