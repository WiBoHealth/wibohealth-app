Moroccan bissara with mint and cumin is a warm, popular North African soup made from split dried fava beans with extra virgin olive oil, garlic, dried mint and cumin. It has a low glycemic index (clinically reviewed estimate ≈35) and is the ideal winter fuel of the workers and farmers of the Atlas.

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

Eat it warm with a spoon, topped with a creamy drizzle of extra virgin olive oil and a pinch of cumin and paprika, as a complete breakfast or lunch without bread.

Best preparation methods

Boil the split fava beans with garlic cloves until they fall apart, purée them smoothly, and add the dried mint, olive oil and cumin in the last few minutes.

Important Nutritional Note

The combination of mint and cumin greatly reduces the bloating fava beans can cause; strict clinical warning: people with favism (G6PD deficiency) must avoid it completely.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the difference between Moroccan and Egyptian bissara?

    Egyptian bissara is green, eaten cold, cooked with dill, molokhia and coriander and scooped up with bread, while Moroccan bissara is a warm yellow soup eaten with a spoon, with olive oil, mint and cumin.

    Is Moroccan bissara suitable for people with diabetes?

    Yes, excellent: its glycemic index is low (≈35), and its high protein and fibre give slow, steady energy without raising blood sugar.

    What is the nutritional value of a bowl of Moroccan bissara (150 g)?

    It contains about 220 kcal, 14 g of plant protein, 11 g of fibre and 4 g of healthy fat from olive oil.
