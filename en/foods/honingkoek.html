Dutch Honey Breakfast Cake (Honingkoek / Ontbijtkoek)

Breads & pastries

Quick answer

A medium portion of Dutch Honey Breakfast Cake (Honingkoek / Ontbijtkoek) (35 g) contains 110 kcal, with 23.5 g of net carbohydrates and 1.2 g of protein. Its glycemic index is 68 (Medium) for how fast it raises blood sugar, while its glycemic load per portion is 16 (Medium) for its actual effect.

Serving advice

Keep to one finger-thick slice (30-35 g). Don't treat it as a breakfast in itself, because it lacks protein; think of it as a sweet treat next to your unsweetened morning coffee.

Best ways to prepare it

The Dutch habit of spreading a thin layer of real butter on top is a smart one: fat slows down stomach emptying, so blood sugar rises more gently.

Important nutrition note ⚠️

For people with metabolic syndrome or belly fat: honingkoek can raise blood sugar and insulin quickly when eaten on its own, and hunger may come back early. Watch the glycemic load carefully.

Frequently asked questions

Is honingkoek healthy for people with diabetes because it contains rye fibre?

It isn't a free everyday choice for people with diabetes. Although a slice has 1.3 g of fibre, its glycemic load is 16 (medium), and its 23.5 g of fast carbohydrate makes blood sugar readings rise quickly.

How can I eat a slice of honingkoek without feeling sluggish or hungry an hour later?

The golden rule: don't eat it on an empty stomach. Have two boiled eggs or a bowl of yogurt first, and spread a little natural peanut butter on top to combine the fibre with protein.

What is the difference between honey breakfast cake (honingkoek) and the regular kind (ontbijtkoek)?

Honey adds a lovely flavour, but it is still a simple sugar (honey's glycemic index is about 58), so it doesn't make the cake healthy.
