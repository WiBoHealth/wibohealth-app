Unsweetened Corn Flakes

Ongezoete cornflakesGrains & starches

Quick answer

A medium portion of Unsweetened Corn Flakes (30 g) contains 108 kcal, with 23.8 g of net carbohydrates and 2.2 g of protein. Its glycemic index is 79 (High) for how fast it raises blood sugar, while its glycemic load per portion is 18.8 (Medium) for its actual effect.

The world's most widespread breakfast cereal: toasted corn flakes with no added sugar. Even without sugar, the heat processing and rolling of the corn make its starch break down and absorb extremely fast, with a high glycemic index (79) according to clinical review.

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

Avoid eating a big bowl with just milk on an empty stomach; mix a small handful into a bowl of Greek yoghurt with chia seeds and nuts to blunt the sharp glucose spike.

Best preparation methods

Always choose plain versions completely free of high-fructose corn syrup or added sugar, and eat them slowly with some protein to slow digestion.

Important Nutritional Note

Even "sugar-free" corn flakes raise blood sugar about as fast as white bread because of their fast starch structure, so whole oats or skyr are far better choices for people with diabetes or watching their weight.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Unsweetened Corn Flakes good for weight loss?

    It can be included in moderation: it contains 3.5 g of fibre per 100 g, which supports fullness, and a 30 g portion gives 108 kcal.

    How does Unsweetened Corn Flakes affect blood sugar?

    Its glycemic index is 79 (High), a clinically reviewed estimate rather than a laboratory measurement. A typical 30 g portion has a glycemic load of 18.8 (Medium), so it is best combined with protein or fibre-rich vegetables.

    What is the nutritional value of a typical portion of Unsweetened Corn Flakes?

    A typical 30 g portion of Unsweetened Corn Flakes provides about 108 kcal, 2.2 g protein, 24.9 g carbohydrate, 0.2 g fat and 1.1 g fibre.
