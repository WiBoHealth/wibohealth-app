Tamarind Drink

Tamarinde drankDrinks

Quick answer

A medium portion of Tamarind Drink (250 g) contains 145 kcal, with 36.2 g of net carbohydrates and 0.8 g of protein. Its glycemic index is 40 (Low) for how fast it raises blood sugar, while its glycemic load per portion is 14.5 (Medium) for its actual effect.

The famous traditional Ramadan drink with a distinctive tang and instant refreshment: an extract of natural tamarind pods. The key question: does tamarind raise blood sugar? Natural tamarind itself is sour with a moderate glycemic index (≈40), but the risk always lies in the large amounts of refined sugar traditionally added to it.

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

Enjoy a cold glass of homemade tamarind drink without sugar, or with a sweetener, to quench your thirst in summer and enjoy its refreshing organic acids.

Best preparation methods

Soak natural tamarind pods in water, boil briefly and strain, then balance the sourness with lemon juice or a few drops of natural stevia instead of white sugar.

Important Nutritional Note

Ready-made tamarind drinks from shops and restaurants are a coloured, concentrated sugar solution that raises blood sugar sharply, so make it at home from pure pods to enjoy it safely.

Frequently asked questions

Is Tamarind Drink good for weight loss?

Yes. Tamarind Drink is very low in calories (58 kcal per 100 g), so you can eat a generous amount to add volume and fullness to a meal without many calories.

How does Tamarind Drink affect blood sugar?

Its glycemic index is 40 (Low), a clinically reviewed estimate rather than a laboratory measurement. A typical 250 g portion has a glycemic load of 14.5 (Medium), so it is best combined with protein or fibre-rich vegetables.

What is the nutritional value of a typical portion of Tamarind Drink?

A typical 250 g portion of Tamarind Drink provides about 145 kcal, 0.8 g protein, 37.5 g carbohydrate, 0.2 g fat and 1.2 g fibre.
