Bruschetta

Bruschetta met tomaat en basilicumItalian dishes

Quick answer

A medium portion of Bruschetta (60 g) contains 86 kcal, with 10.8 g of net carbohydrates and 2.4 g of protein. Its glycemic index is 55 (Low) for how fast it raises blood sugar, while its glycemic load per portion is 5.9 (Low) for its actual effect.

The symbol of simplicity and elegance among Tuscan starters: crisp toasted bread topped with a bright mix of ripe tomatoes, garlic, fragrant basil and extra virgin olive oil. A light, cheerful choice that combines moderate starch with antioxidant power, with a low glycemic index (≈55).

💡 Smart Nutritional Guidance

Consumption advice

One or two pieces of bruschetta are plenty as an appetising starter before the main course, and the healthy fats in extra virgin olive oil help take the edge off strong hunger.

Best preparation methods

Toast the bread well until golden and crisp, as toasting partly changes how quickly its starch breaks down, and rub each slice with a fresh garlic clove before adding the tomato mixture for more flavour.

Important Nutritional Note

Always use whole fresh tomatoes chopped by hand and avoid sweetened tomato paste from a tin, and use cold-pressed extra virgin olive oil to benefit from polyphenols that are good for the blood vessels.

Frequently asked questions

Is Bruschetta good for weight loss?

It can fit a balanced plan with portion control: a 60 g portion gives 86 kcal, and what matters most is your total daily intake and the rest of the meal.

How does Bruschetta affect blood sugar?

Its glycemic index is 55 (Low), a clinically reviewed estimate rather than a laboratory measurement. A typical 60 g portion has a glycemic load of 5.9 (Low), so the effect of this portion on blood sugar is limited.

What is the nutritional value of a typical portion of Bruschetta?

A typical 60 g portion of Bruschetta provides about 86 kcal, 2.4 g protein, 12 g carbohydrate, 3 g fat and 1.2 g fibre.
