### What is psyllium?
Psyllium, or "qatouna" as it is called in Arabic, is the husk of the seeds of a plant called Plantago ovata. It is **almost pure fibre**: there are 85 g of fibre per 100 g!

### How many calories are in psyllium?
**There are 190 calories per 100 g, but a tablespoon (5 g) has about 10 calories and 4 g of fibre.** So one spoon gives you more fibre than a whole apple.

### Does psyllium raise blood sugar?
No. It has almost zero available carbohydrate, because it is mostly fibre that is not digested, so the glycemic index does not apply to it.

### How does it work?
Psyllium absorbs water and becomes like a **gel**. This gel moves through the gut and adds bulk and softness. That is why many people use it for constipation, and also because it gives a feeling of fullness.

### Watch out for these things
- **You must take it with plenty of water**, at least a full glass, otherwise it can get stuck or cause a blockage.
- **Start with a small amount** (half a spoon) and increase gradually, because it can cause bloating at first.
- If you have any health condition, ask your doctor before you start taking it.

### How to use it
- A spoon **in a glass of water or juice**, and drink it quickly before it sets.
- You can add it **to oats or yoghurt**.
- In gluten-free baking, it is used to bind the dough.

### FAQ
**How many calories are in psyllium?**
A tablespoon (5 g) has about 10 calories and 4 g of fibre.

**Does psyllium raise blood sugar?**
No, it is mostly fibre that is not digested.

**How do I take psyllium?**
A spoon in at least a full glass of water, and start with half a spoon.

**Does psyllium help with weight loss?**
It gives a feeling of fullness because it becomes a gel in the stomach, but it does not make you lose weight on its own.
